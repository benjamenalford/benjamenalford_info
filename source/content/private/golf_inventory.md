## II. The Grip

**Interlock or overlap. Neutral. In the fingers.**

### Left Hand (Lead Hand)

1. Hold the club in your right hand at ~45 degree angle
2. Lay the grip across your left hand from **middle of index finger** to **heel pad**
3. Close fingers. Club sits in the **fingers**, NOT deep in the palm
4. Thumb rests **slightly right of center** on top of the grip
5. The **V** formed by thumb and index finger points at your **right ear / right shoulder**
6. You should see **2 to 2.5 knuckles** on your left hand

### Right Hand (Trail Hand)

1. Slide right hand on so grip sits in the **fingers**, not the palm
2. The **lifeline** of your right hand fits over your left thumb like a puzzle piece
3. Right thumb rests **slightly left of center** on the grip
4. The **V** formed by right thumb and index finger also points at your **right ear / right shoulder**
5. **Both Vs pointing the same direction** is the key checkpoint

### Grip Pressure

| Level | Description | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Club falls out | Useless |
| 2-3 | Barely holding on | Too loose |
| **4-5** | **Firm but not tight, like holding a toothpaste tube without squeezing** | **Ideal** |
| 6-7 | White knuckles, tense forearms | Kills wrist hinge, causes slices |
| 8-10 | Death grip | Zero feel, zero power |

**Where pressure lives:**
- Last three fingers of left hand (pinky, ring, middle)
- Middle two fingers of right hand (ring, middle)
- Thumbs and index fingers relatively relaxed, for feel not squeezing

### Common Grip Faults

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Grip in the palm | Club sits deep like a hammer | Move grip into the fingers, runs diagonally across the hand |
| Both Vs at chin | Weak grip, open face, slice | Rotate both hands slightly away until Vs point at right ear |
| Right hand too far under | Strong right hand, hook | Rotate right hand slightly toward target |
| Death grip | Tension | Shake out hands between swings. Pressure 4-5 |
| Thumb straight down top | No support, club twists | Left thumb right of center, right thumb left of center |

**Check your grip before every swing. It takes 3 seconds.**

## III. The Swing Fundamentals

### Setup (Address)

- Feet inside shoulders, ball just forward of center (irons)
- Hips back like sitting on a stool ("bar stool posture")
- Arms hang naturally from shoulders
- Grip Vs point at right ear
- Body lines (feet, hips, shoulders) **parallel-left** of target, like railroad tracks
- Clubface **square to target**, leading edge perpendicular to target line

```
Target line  ──────────────────────►  (clubface aims here)
                    ○  (ball)
Body line    ──────────────────────►  (feet, hips, shoulders aim here — parallel left of target)
```

### The Sequence

**Hips → Torso → Arms → Club. Always.**

| Phase | Hips | Arms |
|---|---|---|
| Backswing | Rotate ~45 deg (shoulders ~90 deg) | Right arm bends ~90 deg, left arm extended, club goes up |
| Transition | Hips start rotating toward target (**the trigger**) | Arms stay back, creates lag |
| Downswing | Hips continue clearing | Arms swing down, deliver the club |
| Impact | Hips ~45 deg open | Clubface square, right arm straightening, ball-first contact |
| Follow-through | Hips face target | Arms over shoulder, balanced finish |

**The hips open the door. The arms walk through it.**

### The Core Principle

**Hit down to make it go up. The iron lifts it. Not you.**

Never scoop. Never flip. The club's loft sends the ball upward when you strike it with a descending blow. Think of skipping a stone — you throw it *down* at the water and it bounces *up*. Same physics.

### The Anti-Chunk Fix

- Weight forward at impact (70% on front foot)
- Ball-first contact — the towel drill teaches this
- Accelerate through the ball — decelerating causes chunks
- Punch shot drill: ball back 2 inches, weight forward, low finish — makes chunking almost impossible

### The Face at Impact

- Face at impact is responsible for **~75-80% of the ball's starting direction**
- Not the swing path. Not the body rotation. The face.
- If the face is square at impact, the ball starts on line

## IV. The Key Drills

### Towel Drill (Modified) — Ball-First Contact

- Fold a towel, place it **2 inches behind the ball** on the rug
- Ball sits directly on the rug (not on the towel)
- Make half-swings trying to hit the ball **without touching the towel**
- If you hit the towel → chunking, hitting behind the ball
- If ball comes clean and towel stays untouched → descending strike, ball-first

### Brush Drill — Low Point Control

- No ball. Just the club and the rug
- Make slow half-swings focusing on **brushing the rug AFTER where the ball would be**
- The brush should happen naturally from your swing arc
- Trains your body that the low point is **in front of the ball**

### Tee Drill — Down and Through

- Place a tee (or coin) where the ball would go
- Place another tee **2 inches in front** of it
- Make half-swings trying to clip the front tee after "hitting" the imaginary ball
- Trains the brain: strike continues down and through, not up and scoop

### Step Drill — Hip-Initiated Sequence

1. Set up to the ball normally
2. Before swinging, lift your lead foot (left) and place it on its toe — balanced on right foot
3. Start your backswing — as you reach the top, **step forward** with your left foot
4. The step triggers hip rotation — hips open as your foot lands
5. Then your arms swing through, delivering the club
6. The step forces the sequence. Hips first, arms second. Every time.

### Punch Shot Drill — Anti-Chunk

1. Set up normally with your 7-iron
2. Move the ball **2 inches back** in your stance (toward back foot)
3. Put **70% of weight on front foot** — lean toward target
4. Make a **3/4 backswing**
5. Swing through with a **low finish** — like punching the ball under a low branch
6. Ball should launch low and run — that's correct

**Why it works:** Weight forward = low point in front of the ball = you can't hit behind it.

### 3:1 Slow-Motion Drill — Sequence & Tempo

- Slow backswing — count "one... two... three"
- Normal downswing — count "one"
- 10 reps. Focus on the sequence: hips start down, then torso, then arms, then club

### Gate Drill (Putting) — Face & Path Control

- Put two coins or bottle caps **4 inches apart**, **6 inches in front of your ball**
- Putt through the gate
- Ball passes clean through = face and path are good
- Ball hits a coin = something's off

### Start-Line Drill (Putting) — Directional Control

- Aim at a target — a shoe, a cup, a mark on the rug
- Focus only on **starting the ball on that line**
- Don't worry about speed — just start line

## V. The Daily Training Plan (15 Minutes)

### Phase 1: Dynamic Warm-Up (4 minutes)

| Exercise | Reps | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| Hip swings (front-to-back) | 5 each leg | Hip rotators |
| Hip openers (gate swing) | 5 each leg | External rotation |
| Overhead squats | 5 reps | Hip mobility + thoracic extension |
| Cross-body shoulder pulls | 5 each side | Shoulder mobility + thoracic rotation |
| Side bends + rotation (with club) | 5 each side | Thoracic spine — #1 limitation |
| Shadow swings (slow, no ball) | 5 reps | Arm-hip dissociation, sequence |

### Phase 2: Swing Practice (9 minutes)

| Drill | Time | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| Punch shot drill (10 reps, no ball) | 1 min | Weight forward, ball-first, anti-chunk |
| Hollow ball full swings (Zing 6-iron) | 3 min | 3/4 to full swings. Watch the ball — pop = good, tumble = chunk, skid = thin, curve = face issue |
| 3:1 slow-motion swings (no ball) | 2 min | Full sequence — hips lead, arms deliver |
| Step drill (10 reps, no ball) | 1 min | Hips initiate, arms follow |
| Putting (Anser 2 + Srixon on rug) | 2 min | Gate drill + start-line drill |

### Hollow Ball Feedback Guide

| Contact | What Happens | What It Means |
|---|---|---|
| Clean strike | Satisfying *pop*, ball flies straight-ish | Face square, ball-first contact |
| Chunk / fat | Ball barely moves or tumbles weakly | Hit the ground/rug first |
| Thin / top | Ball skids low along the floor | Leading edge hit, no loft engaged |
| Slice spin | Ball curves right immediately | Face open or outside-in path |
| Hook spin | Ball curves left immediately | Face closed or inside-out path |

**Hit hollow balls on the floor/rug — NOT on a towel.** The hollow ball IS the feedback. No towel needed.

## VII. Dumbbell Exercises — Scattered Throughout the Day

Keep the 5 lb and 8 lb dumbbells near your desk. Do a set between tasks. No sweat, no shower needed.

### Goblet Squat — Hip Mobility & Glute Power

- Hold one dumbbell vertically against your chest (5 lb → 8 lb)
- Cup the top head with both hands, elbows tucked
- Feet shoulder-width, toes slightly out
- Squat deep — chest up, heels on floor
- Drive up through heels, squeeze glutes at top
- **2 sets of 8 reps** — one morning, one afternoon

### Single-Arm Dumbbell Row — Back & Shoulder Stability

- Left hand and knee on chair/bench, torso horizontal
- Hold dumbbell (8 lb) in right hand, arm hanging straight down
- Pull to hip — elbow tracks past ribcage, not out to side
- Squeeze shoulder blade at top, lower with control
- **2 sets of 8 each arm** — one morning, one afternoon

### Dumbbell Woodchop — Rotational Power & Sequence

- Hold one dumbbell (5 lb) with both hands at right hip
- Feet shoulder-width, slight knee bend
- Rotate hips and torso to drive weight diagonally up to left shoulder — like swinging an axe
- **Movement starts at hips, not arms** — hips rotate first, then torso, then weight follows
- Reverse with control
- **2 sets of 6 each direction** — one morning, one afternoon

**The woodchop before your 15-minute practice session is the most important one — it primes the hip-initiated sequence.**

## IX. Range Day Protocol

**One range session per week. Make it count.**

### Pre-Range (At Home)

- Do the 4-minute dynamic warm-up before you leave
- Pack: Eye 2s in Tourbon bag, Srixon Soft Feels, tees, towel from home, Anser 2

### At the Range

| Step | Balls | Club | Focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Warm up | 0 | None | Dynamic warm-up at your bay |
| 2. Find contact | 10 | PW or 9-iron | Half-swings only. Find the click |
| 3. Build the swing | 10 | 7-iron | 3/4 swings. Sequence — hips lead, arms deliver |
| 4. Full swings | 10 | 7-iron | Full swings. Pick a target every shot |
| 5. Mid-iron | 5 | 5-iron or 6-iron | Smooth tempo |
| 6. Long iron | 5 | 4-iron or 3-iron | Smooth tempo, don't swing harder |
| 7. Putting green | 5 min | Anser 2 | Lag putts + short putts |

**Total: ~50 balls, ~45 minutes**

### Range Rules

- **One ball at a time** — step back, reset, check grip, set up, swing
- **No rapid fire** — one ball every 30-45 seconds
- **Pick a target** — every shot. No hitting into the void
- **No driver** — not yet. Months away
- **Start with short irons** — always. PW first, work up
- **If contact falls apart** — go back to half-swings with PW

### Range Etiquette

- Don't stand close to someone who's swinging
- Don't talk during someone's backswing
- If you hit a ball into someone's bay, say sorry
- Pick up your tees and trash
- Check the dress code — most ranges expect a collared shirt

## XII. The Glossary

### The 10 Terms That Matter Most Right Now

1. **Grip** — in the fingers, 2-2.5 knuckles, Vs at right ear, pressure 4-5
2. **Address** — your setup before the swing
3. **Descending strike** — hit down to make it go up
4. **Ball-first contact** — hit the ball before the ground
5. **Sequence** — hips → torso → arms → club
6. **Slice** — ball curves hard left-to-right (your likely miss)
7. **Chunk / Fat** — hitting the ground before the ball
8. **Thin / Top** — hitting the ball with the leading edge
9. **Follow-through** — hold your finish 3 seconds
10. **Pure** — the perfectly struck shot. You'll know it when you hit it.

### The Swing

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Address | Setup position before the swing |
| Takeaway | First movement of the club away from the ball |
| Backswing | Moving the club from address to the top |
| Transition | Change of direction from backswing to downswing — where sequence starts |
| Downswing | Moving the club from the top to the ball |
| Impact | The moment the clubface meets the ball |
| Follow-through / Finish | Continuation after impact — your report card |
| Lag | Angle between lead arm and shaft on downswing — more lag = more power |
| Release | Unhinging of wrists through impact — the whip cracks |
| Tempo | Rhythm and timing — not how fast, how smooth and sequenced |
| Loft | Angle of clubface relative to vertical — determines launch height |
| Lie angle | Angle between shaft and ground when club is soled properly |
| Swing plane | Imaginary tilted circle the club travels on |
| Swing path | Direction the clubhead is traveling at impact |
| Clubface | The striking surface of the club |
| Leading edge | Bottom edge of the clubface |
| Sole | Bottom of the clubhead |
| Toe | Far end of the clubhead (away from you) |
| Heel | Near end of the clubhead (closest to you) |
| Sweet spot | Center of the clubface — maximum ball speed, zero twisting |
| Descending strike | Club still moving downward at impact — required for irons |
| Ascending strike | Hitting the ball on the upswing — only for driver off a tee |
| Ball-first contact | Striking the ball before the ground |
| Fat / Chunk | Hitting the ground before the ball |
| Thin / Top | Hitting the ball with the leading edge — ball stays low |
| Scoop / Flip | Trying to lift the ball by flipping wrists at impact |

## XIII. Club Storage & Care

### Until the Bag Arrives

- Stand clubs upright in a room corner
- Heads resting on a towel
- Putter in the middle of the group
- Never against a wall, never flat on the floor, never in the garage

### When the Bag Arrives

- Long irons (3, 4) in the back (farthest from strap)
- Short irons and wedges in the front (closest to strap)
- Putter in its own slot/divider
- Ping Vault cover on the Anser 2 at all times when not in use

### General Care

- Wipe clubs down after use — moisture rusts steel shafts
- Never leave clubs in a hot car — heat weakens shaft epoxy
- Never lean clubs against a wall — they fall and dent
- Replace grips when they feel slippery or look worn

## XV. The Mental Game

- **Golf is not cycling.** Effort ≠ result. Trying harder often makes it worse.
- **One shot at a time.** The last one is over. The next one doesn't exist yet.
- **Your worst enemy is expectations.** You will be terrible at first. Every golfer was.
- **The 7-iron is your reset button.** When everything falls apart, grab it and hit half-swings until you find contact.
- **Leave the driver in the bag when you're struggling.** 3-iron off the tee in play > driver in the woods.
- **A proper iron shot takes a divot.** After the ball. That's not a chunk — that's correct.
